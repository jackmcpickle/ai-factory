Ahead of our session, here's the technical case study we'd like you to work through. Please send us your
working files before we meet and come ready to walk us through your thinking and change parts of it live.


The role you're stepping into


You're joining as Senior Manager, Applied AI, responsible for the Product Innovation Centre's Agentic
Product Development Lifecycle Centre of Excellence and Operating Model. Your team sets the standard for
this new way of working and demonstrates the best practice the rest of the Product organisation will adopt.


The Centre includes Product Managers, Platform and Product engineers (backend Java, mobile backend
Kotlin, iOS, Android, front end React, Salesforce), Quality Engineers, Product Designers, Iteration
Managers, and leadership roles across Chapter Leads, Change Management, and People and Culture.


Our strategy is to run slimmer teams augmented by agents. A traditional squad here runs to about ten
people. We're testing what a materially smaller, agent-augmented squad looks like in practice, not just in
theory, and this case study is one of the ways we test that.


The Centre's products include the Qantas App, the Qantas.com booking flow and public website, and the
Contact Centre agent experience.


The problem


The team is currently working on a new capability to let customers pre-order their meals within the Qantas
App. Customers often miss out on their preferred meal choice, there's meaningful wastage in meal options,
especially in premium cabins, and we have limited visibility into how meals are actually consumed onboard.


The task


We want you to design the AI-augmented delivery workflow your team would run to take this idea from
inception through to production, not a customer-facing meal-ordering agent. The feature itself may show up
inside your workflow as evidence of what it produces, but the thing we're assessing is how you'd use AI to
run discovery, design, build, test, and launch for this initiative, end to end.


Use whatever AI tools you'd naturally reach for. We primarily use Claude Code and GitHub Copilot at the
Centre.


Please cover:


    •   The workflow breakdown. What are the stages, what runs with an agent in the loop, and what
        stays human-only, and why.
    •   Context, skills, and tools. What context, skills, and tooling does your workflow need, and how
        would you design and maintain them.
    •   Quality. How would you know your workflow's outputs, at each stage, are good enough to trust.
        Not "it looked right when I ran it."
    •   Risk and guardrails. Pick the one step in your workflow that touches something irreversible or
        safety-relevant, think dietary accuracy, a booking or catering commitment, and show us the
        guardrail you'd put around it.
    •   Team shape. Given our strategy of slimmer, agent-augmented squads, what does the team for
        this specific initiative look like, and how does that compare to a traditional ten-person squad. What
        changes about the roles that remain.
    •   Cost and observability. What does this cost to run, where would you look if it started costing
        more than expected, and what would you actually see if you needed to debug it.
    •   Rollout. The team currently working on this product has a mixed level of AI tooling adoption. How
        would you roll this workflow out to them, with support from Change and Delivery Management, and
        how would you know it's landing.
    •   Tradeoffs and prioritisation. What did you prioritise, what did you deliberately leave out, and
        why.


Talk us through your design decisions and the reasoning behind them as you go. We'll spend the second
half of the session pushing on specific parts of it, and we'll ask you to change aspects of it live.


Time. This should take a strong candidate three to four hours of focused work, with AI tooling doing most of
the heavy lifting. If it's taking a lot longer than that, scope down rather than pushing through.


What to send us before we meet.

A working version of your workflow, and a zip file with the full structure and any supporting files, prompts,
configs, agent definitions, sample outputs. Let us know what you'll need to demo it live, your own machine,
a screen share, or something we can run ourselves, so we can plan for that.
